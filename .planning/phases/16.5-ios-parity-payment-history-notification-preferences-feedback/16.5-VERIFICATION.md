### WR-01 assessment: Cancel Booking on a past confirmed booking from Payment History

**Verdict: a real but small hardening gap relative to D-03's intent; not a goal gap. Warning, not a blocker.**

Evidence:
- The code is as the review says: `canCancel = status in CANCELLABLE_BOOKING_STATUSES` (`UpcomingBookingUiState.kt:108`) with no time check, while Join (`showsJoinCard`) and Reschedule (`canClientStartReschedule(..., end, now)`) are time-gated. The backend `cancelBooking` guards status only, per the review.
- **D-03** says a row tap opens Upcoming Booking "for every row, as on iOS", and asks research to confirm the screen "offers no live action (Join, Reschedule) on a booking where it can't apply". The enumerated actions are Join and Reschedule; Cancel is not named, though "no live action" is the stated principle. So WR-01 is outside the letter of D-03 and inside its spirit.
- **iOS comparison** (`Upcoming Booking Details/UpcomingBookingView.swift:127-131`): iOS renders `UpcomingBookingSecondaryActions` (Reschedule and Cancel) unconditionally for every booking, with no status or time check at all. Android is already strictly safer than iOS: it hides Cancel for completed, cancelled, declined, missed, expired and unknown statuses and only exposes it for `confirmed`, `pending_approval`, `pending_reschedule`. The remaining exposure is a `confirmed` booking whose end has passed without a status transition, which is a data edge case, not the normal ledger row.
- The phase goal is "match iOS", and Cancel on such a row matches (and is narrower than) iOS. No success criterion in ROADMAP or any plan's must_haves requires time-gating Cancel.

Recommendation: record a disposition. Either defer to a polish item (low risk, and the review itself says to confirm with iOS and the product owner first, since iOS offers it) or fix with the review's `canCancelNow` suggestion plus a test beside `terminalStatusesShowNoLiveAction`. It should not block moving to Phase 16. Currently `16.5-REVIEW-DISPOSITION.md` lists all five findings as `open`.
